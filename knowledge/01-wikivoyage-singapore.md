# Districts, attractions and food

Singapore’s central areas include the Riverside/Civic District, Orchard, Marina Bay, Bugis and Kampong Glam, Chinatown and Little India. The outer areas include the East Coast and Sentosa. Chinatown is a place to seek Chinese food and cultural experiences; Little India is associated with Indian flavours; Geylang Serai is associated with Malay culture; Katong and the East Coast have Peranakan, Eurasian and seafood experiences.

For major sights, Marina Bay includes Gardens by the Bay. The Botanic Gardens are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Mandai Wildlife Reserve includes Singapore Zoo, Night Safari, Bird Paradise and River Wonders. Sentosa is a resort island with beaches and attractions. Museums and historic buildings are concentrated around Bras Basah and the colonial core.

# Moving around and practical guidance

Singapore is compact but tropical heat and humidity can make walking tiring; carry water and an umbrella. Main business-district pavements and crossings are generally good. Public transport is useful for getting around the island. English is widely used, alongside Mandarin, Malay and Tamil. Hawker centres and coffee shops are notable places to explore food.

# Three-day direction

A three-day visit can combine food, culture and shopping. A sensible generated plan can group nearby areas: Marina Bay and the civic core; Chinatown, Little India and Kampong Glam; then Sentosa, East Coast or a wildlife/nature day. Check individual attraction opening hours and tickets directly with operators before travel.
